Know where the fabric goes.

A simple daily log for a garment factory in Ethiopia. Write down the fabric issued and the garments finished. The tracker works out the yield and the output per worker, and points out the days that look wrong.

The last 30 days

Why it exists

Fabric

Fabric is bought and handed to production for every run. Nobody compared the metres issued with the garments that came out, so waste or loss could go unnoticed.

Output

Nobody counted how many pieces each line or worker finished. Productivity was judged by feel, which makes it hard to know whether a change in team size actually helped.

How it works

1. Record

At the end of the day, one person enters the fabric issued, the garments finished and the number of workers for each line.

2. Compare

The tracker divides the fabric the garments should have used by the fabric actually issued. That is the yield. It also shows garments per worker per day.

3. Act

A batch with a low yield, or a day with a sharp drop in output, shows up in a list. Someone can then go and find out why.

The trial

The plan is to log every new batch for four to six weeks next to the normal routine. One or two people enter the numbers, the numbers are reviewed every week, and the factory decides afterwards whether to keep using it. Before the busy season starts, the output numbers can be used to plan staffing.

Every change to an entry is kept on the History page, with the old and new values, so nothing is lost if a number is corrected by mistake.

About the project

This is an independent research project from 2026. It began as a study of one small garment manufacturer in Ethiopia that buys imported fabric and makes clothing for local and wholesale buyers. The company is not named here, to keep the business and its people private.

Fabric yield by day

Yield is the fabric the finished garments should have used, divided by the fabric actually issued. 100% means exactly the standard. Points below the red line need a look.

Garments per worker by day

Garments finished divided by the number of workers on those lines that day. The dashed line is the average for this period.
